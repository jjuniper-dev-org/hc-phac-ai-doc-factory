# SRC-SSC-CANCHAT-CANADA-CA-2026 — CANChat - Canada.ca

## Source identity

- Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/shared-services/services/gc-ai/canchat.html
- Publisher: Shared Services Canada (SSC).
- Page details date shown on page: 2026-06-19.
- Stated scope: Public Canada.ca page describing CANChat, a generative AI chatbot for the Government of Canada (GC).
- Relevance to AI document factory: Primary public-facing source on CANChat's purpose, sovereignty framing, LLM offering, and deployment status — directly relevant to the GoC AI platform landscape intel brief (INTEL-004).

## Source-backed facts

- CANChat is described as a generative AI chatbot designed for the Government of Canada.
- The page describes generative AI generally as a type of artificial intelligence that can create new content, in addition to analyzing or sorting existing information, learning from large amounts of text, images, and sounds.
- CANChat is presented as built in and operated in Canada by the Canadian government, for use by the Canadian federal public service (per Joshua Turner, Manager, Data Science and Artificial Intelligence, SSC, in the page transcript).
- The page states a key consideration in CANChat's development was sovereignty — ensuring creation and operation of the technology took place in Canada, operated by Canadians, and subject only to Canadian law.
- The page lists benefits for Canadians: speeding up service delivery, improving access to accurate information, enabling better/faster decisions, and freeing up public servants' time for complex cases and program improvement.
- The page states these benefits come "all while keeping Canadian data secure and digitally sovereign."
- The page states CANChat offers a variety of LLMs to use, including models that are Canadian-built and Canadian-hosted.
- The page states the rationale for Canadian-built/Canadian-hosted models is to ensure outputs reflect Canadian context, values and priorities, reduce reliance on foreign technologies, strengthen self-determination and data control, and promote secure, unbiased AI services.
- The page states SSC has begun deployment of CANChat with "some GC departments and agencies" (unnamed).
- The page states a phased approach to expand the user base is planned, starting in July 2026, until full GC coverage is achieved.
- The page explicitly caveats: "CANChat doesn't think or understand like a human. It predicts what comes next based on patterns, but it can make mistakes and still needs review."
- The page references related stories ("CANChat: SSC's first generative AI chatbot," "SSC's CANChat brings innovation to life at CIRNAC") and a GCXchange page (accessible only on the GC network) for training.

## Working interpretation

For HC/PHAC platform-landscape purposes, this page positions CANChat as a workforce-productivity, multi-model chat surface with an explicit sovereignty narrative (Canadian-built/Canadian-hosted model options, data residency, Canadian legal jurisdiction). It reads as an enterprise-wide GC productivity tool rather than a program-specific or health-domain tool. The July 2026 phased expansion language suggests CANChat's footprint is still growing and department-level adoption (including at HC/PHAC) cannot be assumed from this page alone. This source strengthens the "productivity interface" / "workforce productivity surface" framing already used for CANChat in INTEL-004, and adds sovereignty and Canadian-hosted-model framing that was not previously source-backed.

## Evidence gaps

- The page does not state that CANChat is deployed at Health Canada (HC) or the Public Health Agency of Canada (PHAC) specifically — it only refers to "some GC departments and agencies," unnamed.
- The page does not state any Protected B (or other data classification) approval or operational-data approval for CANChat.
- The page does not state any relationship between CANChat and PATH, HAIL, or M365 Copilot — no integration, comparison, or convergence claim appears on this page.
- The page does not name which specific Canadian-built/Canadian-hosted LLMs are offered.
- The page does not specify which departments/agencies have been onboarded as of the page date, nor a target full-GC-coverage completion date beyond "starting in July 2026."
