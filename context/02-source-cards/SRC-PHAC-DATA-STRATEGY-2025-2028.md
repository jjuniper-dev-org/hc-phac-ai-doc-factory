## Source-backed facts

- The strategy is PHAC’s renewed Data Strategy for 2025/26–2027/28.
- The strategy focuses on health data rather than all corporate data.
- PHAC frames modern, secure, timely health data as foundational to safeguarding and improving public health.
- PHAC identifies persistent challenges in collecting, sharing, accessing, and using health data.
- PHAC identifies inconsistencies in data sharing, varying data standards, and limited integration as barriers to efficiency, flexibility, and responsiveness.
- PHAC operates more than 50 active surveillance systems relying on more than 180 sources.
- Among frequently reported data providers, provinces and territories represent 64 percent and hospitals or other healthcare settings represent 28 percent.
- The strategy has four priorities: establish effective data governance; connect data and systems; enhance culture of data stewardship; foster a data-literate workforce.
- Priority 1 explicitly includes decision making on data-driven issues such as data sharing, interoperability, disaggregated data, technology, and AI integration.
- Priority 1 calls for collaboration with HC-DTB to ensure a public-health-focused data architecture.
- Priority 2 emphasizes interoperable-by-design data systems, standards, simplified external data intake, data linkage, and established platforms/tools.
- Priority 3 emphasizes FAIR and CARE principles, open science, open data, data stewardship, responsible exchange, and privacy safeguards.
- Priority 4 emphasizes data literacy, common data tools, career development, communities of practice, and talent development in data and AI.
- The strategy highlights Indigenous data sovereignty, including CARE and OCAP principles.
- The strategy highlights data bias, SGBA+, anti-racism in science, disaggregated data, and equity-informed data practices.
- The afterword explicitly states that PHAC must strengthen core data practices and systems and formalize AI best practices in data governance structures to responsibly leverage AI for public health action.
- The evaluation plan includes annual reports in 2026, 2027, and 2028, with a comprehensive mixed-methods evaluation in 2029.

## Working interpretation

This source strengthens the AI readiness position: PHAC’s ability to scale AI for public health depends on data governance, interoperability, stewardship, standards, literacy, and equity-informed practices. AI should therefore be assessed as part of data modernization and public-health architecture, not as a separate tool-only agenda.
