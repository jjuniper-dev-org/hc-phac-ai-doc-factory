## Source-backed facts

- The page defines digital sovereignty as Canada staying in control of its own digital future — including its data, technology, and essential online services — instead of relying on foreign companies, foreign systems, or foreign laws.
- The page frames this with an analogy: just as Canadians want their homes, money, and identities protected in Canada, the same protection is wanted for digital information and services.
- The page states "data stays protected at home": Canadians' information is handled under Canadian laws, not rules set in another country.
- The page states Canada "stays flexible and resilient": if global rules change or international systems go down, Canada remains in control and can keep services running.
- The page lists related links: "Digital sovereignty," "Digital Sovereignty: A Framework to improve digital readiness of the Government of Canada," "Canadian Sovereign AI Compute Strategy," and "Shared Services Canada 2026–27 Departmental Plan."
